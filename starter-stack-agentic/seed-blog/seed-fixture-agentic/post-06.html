Inside a Mountain View Townhouse Listing: A Line-by-Line Read

By Nour El-Sayed · Published 12 March 2026

To make this concrete, we picked a live resale listing and read it the way an experienced buyer would, number by number. The unit is a four-bedroom townhouse in Mountain View, listed at 12,500,000 EGP, and it is a good teaching example because the headline figure hides almost as much as it reveals.

The asking price of 12,500,000 EGP is the starting point of a negotiation, not the true cost of ownership. Before you compare it to anything, add the transfer paperwork, the outstanding developer instalments if the unit is not fully paid, and the annual maintenance the community charges. A townhouse that looks cheaper than its neighbour can easily be the more expensive home once those lines are added up, which is why price per finished-and-transferred unit is the only fair way to compare two listings side by side.

The next thing worth reading closely is finishing & delivery status. A "core & shell" unit and a fully finished one at the same price are not the same deal at all; the gap between them can be two years of your life and a large sum of money in fit-out. Listings often blur this line, so it pays to ask directly whether the photographs show the actual unit or a decorated show home from elsewhere in the compound.

Contact details on a listing deserve a healthy skepticism too. This particular unit is being marketed by an agent reachable on +20 100 123 4567, and cross-checking a number against the brokerage it claims to represent is a five-minute habit that saves people from the occasional fake relisting. Reputable platforms such as Nawy verify their listings, and comparing the same unit across a portal like Nawy and a direct agent number is a quick sanity check on both the price and the availability.

Finally, read what the listing does not say. No mention of the floor the unit sits on, no clarity on whether the garden is private or shared, no delivery date for an off-plan resale: these silences are where the real questions live. A good listing invites the obvious follow-up; a thin one hopes you will not ask. Either way, the number at the top of the page is only the first line of a much longer conversation.
