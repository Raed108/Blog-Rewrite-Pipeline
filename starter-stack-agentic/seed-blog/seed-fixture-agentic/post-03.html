Reading a Floor Plan Before You Ever Visit the Unit

By Karim Fahmy · Published 19 February 2026

A floor plan looks like a technical drawing, but for a buyer it is really a story about how a day would feel inside the home. Learn to read it and you can shorten a viewing list from twenty units to five before you spend a single Friday driving between compounds. The trick is to stop looking at the total area and start looking at how that area is arranged.

Start with circulation: the corridors, entry, and the paths people walk to get from one room to another. Space spent on wide hallways is space you paid for but cannot furnish. A well-drawn plan moves you from the front door to the living area without marching you past three bedroom doors, and it keeps the kitchen close to where deliveries and bins go rather than at the far end of the home.

Next, check the orientation and where the windows fall. A plan will usually mark north, and in Cairo that matters enormously: a living room facing the harsh afternoon sun behaves very differently from one that catches gentle morning light. Some developers even brand a particular layout, and in one project the sun-managed corner unit is marketed under the label منتج-زد٩, a name you will see repeated across the sales material for that specific configuration.

Then look at the walls that cannot move. Columns, shear walls, and wet-area stacks are fixed; everything else is, in principle, negotiable in a renovation. Buyers often fall for a layout they intend to "open up" later, only to discover the wall between the kitchen and living room is structural. Reading which lines are load-bearing before you fall in love saves a lot of disappointment.

Finally, imagine the furniture. A bedroom that measures well on paper can still refuse a real bed once you allow for a door swing and a wardrobe. Sketch the largest pieces you own into each room in your head. If they do not fit on the drawing, they will not fit in the flat, and no amount of clever staging at the showroom will change that once you have moved in.
