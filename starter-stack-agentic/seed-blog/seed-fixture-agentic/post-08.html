Three Documents to Check Before You Sign a Reservation

By Omar Tawfik · Published 1 March 2026

A reservation form feels harmless. It is usually short, the deposit is modest against the price of the home, and the salesperson is warm and encouraging. But a reservation is still a commitment, and the details that matter are almost never on the form itself. They are in the documents the form quietly refers to, and a calm hour reading them before you sign is worth far more than any pressure to lock in the unit today.

The three you should never skip

  • The payment schedule, in full, so you know exactly what is due, when, and what happens to your deposit if you walk away before the contract is signed.
  • The unit specification sheet, which lists finishing level, area, and delivery date in language you can hold the developer to later.
  • The draft sale contract, or at least its key clauses on penalties, delays, and transfer, so nothing in it surprises you after the deposit has cleared.

None of these is exotic, and a serious developer will hand them over without fuss. If a salesperson is reluctant to share the draft contract before you reserve, treat that reluctance as information. You can read more about your rights as an off-plan buyer on the Forsa buyer guides, but the shortest version is simple: never sign for what you have not read, no matter how good the unit looks in the afternoon light.
